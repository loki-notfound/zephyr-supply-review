Is Zephyr’s supply right? Full report

Version 1.0, 2026-11-14. The supply incident of 2023 to 2025, its cause, the audit and the chain since. An independent review, made on the author’s own initiative for the Zephyr community.

Rule of this report: nothing is claimed that cannot be checked. Every finding names its basis and, where possible, the command or file that reproduces it (§10, and the evidence register in §1a). Inferences are labelled as inferences. What we could not establish is listed in §1b.

This report separates three kinds of statement and marks each:

  • chain read from a full copy of the Zephyr main chain, or from a synced node, on 2026-11-14;
  • code read from the public source repositories, with commit and line;
  • stated said by the project, an exchange or a mining service, quoted from a text we read in full: release notes, notices and pages on the web, the project’s Discord, read from transcripts of four of its channels and searches of the rest and cited as stated, Discord with the date, and the project’s posts on X, read from transcripts of selected posts and of searches of the account and cited as stated, X. All of them are listed in §11.

Nothing in this report rests on a text that was not read in full.

Where the evidence allows more than one explanation, all are given. Times are in UTC where the source gives one; a commit is dated by its author date in UTC, and a web page that shows only a date keeps that date (§11).

1. The question and the answer

The question. Is the supply of ZEPH right? Taken apart, that is five questions that public data can answer. This review establishes what happened and what the supply is; it does not judge who is to blame.

  1. Can the number the chain shows be relied on?Yes, as a count, up to block 902083 (2026-11-07), where our copy of the chain ends. Up to that block every transaction passes the checks that keep a transaction from creating coins (range proof and balance, conversion amount, ring signatures), no key image occurs twice, and no block pays out more than the reward formula and the fees allow (§5.4). The totals of all four assets agree with the coins generated and the public conversions at both points where they can be compared, right after the audit and at that last block (§6.3, §6.3a). What such checks cannot exclude is a flaw in the rules themselves that nobody knows of (question 5).
  2. What went wrong before the audit?Two defects in consensus code that Zephyr took over from Haven Protocol (§3, §4). Range proofs were not verified until 2024-10-13, so a transaction could create coins; ring members could be of another asset until 2025-01-23, so one asset could be spent as another. Both were used: three transactions with invalid range proofs and fourteen that spent a stable-coin output as ZEPH are on the chain (§5). Both defects are closed.
  3. What could the audit do, and what could it not?It made the supply countable for the first time: every holder moved each asset once with the amount in the open (§6.2). It could not separate coins that had been emitted from coins that had not, because amounts and histories are hidden and the chain offers no way to tell them apart. After the audit the supply table held 2,067,576 ZEPH more than had been emitted: 1,921,650 minted once for an exchange balance that was not audited in time, and 145,926 inside the audited supply (§6.3, §6.4). The project announced the mint and how it is offset: it was booked as emission, so block rewards are about 18 % lower for as long as they follow the emission curve, about ten more years. By a projection that offsets all but about 124,000 ZEPH of the mint before the fixed tail reward takes over. The 145,926 are not offset.
  4. What stays unknown?How many coins each defect produced, who made the transactions, and whether the unaudited exchange balance consisted of emitted coins (§1b). On the accounting of §6.5, defect B can account for at most about 262,688 ZEPH of the excess. For the rest the project’s own report says: “We were not able to determine if or how much net chain-wide inflation originated from this vector” stated, X, 2025-09-06.
  5. Has anyone outside the project reviewed the node’s code?Not that we could find. In 2025 the project said no third-party review was planned; the audit by the firm Sherlock that it commissioned in 2026 covers its EVM bridge, by the scope it published, not the node; in the project’s words, “Scoped to the EVM bridge only ... Not the L1” (§8). This review is not a code audit either: it checks the chain against the rules as they are written.

In short. Up to the block we checked, the number the chain shows is a sound count. At the end of the audit (block 536000) it was 2,067,576 ZEPH higher than ordinary emission to that block: 1,921,650 that the project minted for an exchange balance that was not audited, for the larger part offset by lower block rewards in the years after, and 145,926 among the audited coins, which the audit had no way to tell from the rest.

The findings in summary.

  1. Zephyr’s multi-asset code was taken from Haven Protocol in May 2023 code. Two defects in the consensus rules came with it or were made in adapting it; the project wrote in August 2025 that “both Zephyr exploits were inherited from “audited” Haven code portions” stated, X:
    • A. Range proofs of transactions were not verified from the launch (2023-05-29) until hard fork 6 (block 360000, 2024-10-13). A range proof is what prevents a transaction from creating coins. Haven’s fix of 2024-12-11, titled “fix range proof exploit”, adds the missing verification to the same function. code, executed test
    • B. Ring members were not required to have the asset type the input claimed until hard fork 7 (block 433000, 2025-01-23). An output of one asset could be spent as another asset with the same amount (“asset morphing”). code
  2. Transactions that only these defects made possible are on the main chain chain:
    • A: exactly three transactions with invalid range proofs were accepted, in blocks 289260 and 289262 (2024-07-07, six minutes apart). No other transaction up to block 902083 (§5.4) has an invalid proof. The project itself wrote on 2025-01-23 of “three suspect transactions that fail the updated range proofs code” stated, Discord.
    • B: 14 transactions in which an input claiming ZEPH has a ring made up entirely of stable-coin (ZSD) outputs, from 2023-10-29 to 2024-09-30, so that a stable-coin output was necessarily spent as ZEPH; and 16 further transactions (35 inputs) with one or two ring members of another asset, which admit two readings, a decoy or a real spend; the rings do not decide between them (§5.3). Together that is 30 transactions, the number the project gave on 2025-01-23 for “transactions ... with cross-asset ring members” stated, Discord. The amounts are hidden by design. The chain does not show how many coins these transactions created, nor who made them.
  3. The project closed B with a hotfix (v2.0.2), then ran a chain-wide audit (hard forks 8 to 11, 2025-03-31 to 2025-06-15) in which every holder had to move each asset once with a public amount. Coins not moved became invalid.
  4. Result, reconciled by us against the coins the node recorded as generated chain, code: after the audit the node’s supply table records 9,749,946 ZEPH where 7,682,370 had been emitted: 2,067,576 more than was emitted. The project’s own estimate of 2025-06-11 is +2,056,803 stated, Discord. Of that, 1,921,650 ZEPH is a one-time mint in block 536000 which the release notes and the project’s announcements attribute to an exchange balance that was not audited (MEXC) stated; 145,926 ZEPH is excess inside the audited supply itself (audited coins plus the recorded reserve). Coins that exist beyond what was generated were not emitted; genuine coins that were never audited reduce the visible excess, so the amount of non-emitted coins honoured is at least this figure. The reserve it depends on was rebuilt from genesis and agrees with the node’s record to within 0.001 ZEPH (§6.3a).
  5. By the project’s estimate the other assets came in 256,634 units short in total (ZSD, ZRS, ZYS); rebuilt from the chain, 262,688 units are missing (§6.3a). Defect B turns one unit of another asset into one ZEPH, so it cannot account for more than about that much of the ZEPH excess; the project’s own estimate for it is about 200,000. At least about 1.8 million of the excess is therefore not explained by defect B. Defect A is the only other known cause, but nothing shows how much the three transactions created (§6.5). The project’s post-audit report says the same of that cause: “We were not able to determine if or how much net chain-wide inflation originated from this vector” stated, X, 2025-09-06.
  6. The one-time mint was booked as emission. The parameter that sets the emission curve is unchanged, so block rewards are about 18 % lower than on the original schedule until the fixed tail reward is reached code, chain. By a projection without penalties that offsets about 1.8 million ZEPH of the mint; about 124,000 of it, and the 145,926, stay above the original schedule (§6.4). The lower rewards are borne by miners, the reserve share and the yield share.
  7. Both defects are closed in the current release (v2.3.0, published 2025-06-11). The public repository’s only branch still ends at the commit of that release (checked 2026-11-14) code.

1b. What this report does not establish

  • How many coins the three invalid-proof transactions or the morphing transactions created.
  • Who made them, and whether one party or several.
  • Which defect accounts for how much of the excess.
  • Whether the coins in the unaudited exchange balance had been emitted or not. (Why they were not audited, how the balance was checked and that it was paid out are stated by the project, §6.4; we cannot verify those statements on chain.)
  • Whether the sixteen partial-ring transactions are abuse (§5.3).
  • The intent behind the correction of defect A in v2.0.0. The project says it was a by-product of refactoring and that the earlier check was an oversight (§4.1); the code is consistent with that.
  • How many genuine coins were lost because their holders did not audit them.
  • That no third defect existed. Every transaction passes the checks of the released code that we re-ran (§5.4), but a defect in those checks themselves, as A and B were, would not show that way, and not every consensus rule was re-run (§2).
  • What the project said on Medium, or in the parts of its website, its Discord and its X account we did not read (§11). None of that is used here.

2. Method and limits

This review was made with AI assistance (Claude Fable 5.2, Anthropic); the README says how. The programs and their results are published so that no finding depends on trusting the author or the model.

Sources. The repositories ZephyrProtocol/zephyr (tag v2.3.0, 67c5f53b), haven-protocol-org/haven-main (tip of the main branch 70d01079, 2024-12-11), the release notes of all Zephyr releases (GitHub API), Zephyr’s whitepaper, a full Zephyr chain database synced from genesis, and a second, separately synced node for RPC queries and cross-checks.

The node that synced the database was built from the zephyr-backports fork, tag v2.3.0-sec.2 (https://github.com/zephyr-backports/zephyr): v2.3.0 with security fixes taken over from Monero (memory safety, denial of service, dependency updates). These fixes do not change which blocks or transactions are valid. That the database holds the same chain as the original software was checked directly: its block hashes at heights 289262, 360000, 433000, 481500, 536000 and at its last block, 902083 (c2e6d09f5eeb37453fe34a1245fc6bb92a137441375256acf120da8ef1338eba), are identical to those of a separately synced node running the official v2.3.0 binary. A block hash commits to every earlier block and every transaction in them, so the two chains are identical up to that block. The scan programs were built against the libraries of an unmodified v2.3.0 build. Anyone can repeat the scan on a database synced with the stock release and compare the hash above first.

Code comparison. A script (heritage.py) takes the lines that Zephyr’s first own commit adds to the C and C++ sources, collapses whitespace, leaves out lines shorter than 25 characters, comment lines and lines that already occur in the commit’s parent (Monero), and counts how many of the rest occur anywhere in Haven’s sources of the same day (211ddae93).

Executed test of defect A. A program linked against the libraries built from v2.3.0 takes a real transfer from block 300002, calls both verification functions, flips one bit in the range proof and calls them again:

height 300002 hf 5 tx 4f9693d5b598435f33f00b15ba62c3a433caae2c9108dd73d994bf6161504e89 outputs 2
original proof : bulletproof_plus_VERIFY=1  verRctSemanticsSimple(pre-HF6 path)=1  verRctSemanticsZeph(HF6+ path)=1
corrupted proof: bulletproof_plus_VERIFY=0  verRctSemanticsSimple(pre-HF6 path)=1  verRctSemanticsZeph(HF6+ path)=0

Chain scan. A read-only program walks every block, parses every non-coinbase transaction and (1) looks up, for each ring member of each input, the asset type stored with that output and compares it with the asset type the input claims; (2) verifies the Bulletproof+ range proofs with the library’s own verifier. Below block 435000, the scan’s first range boundary after hard fork 7 (block 433000): 837,680 transactions, 4,842,712 inputs, 77,483,392 ring members, 837,680 proofs. The rest of the chain is scanned as a control (§5.4). The asset type of a ring member is the one the node stored when the output was created; for two of the fourteen full-ring transactions all sixteen members were looked up again on the second node through the transactions that created them, with the same result.

Re-validation. A second read-only program re-runs, for every non-coinbase transaction, the checks the released code applies at the transaction’s height: the semantics check (verRctSemanticsSimple below hard fork 6, verRctSemanticsZeph from it; for conversions with the pricing record of the block the transaction names), the minted amount of conversions below hard fork 6 (validateMintedAmount), and the ring signatures and commitments (verRctNonSemanticsSimple) with the rings read from the chain. A transaction that fails the semantics check under its block’s fork version is tried once more under the previous version and would be reported with a note, and so would a minted amount that passes only under the previous version; no such case occurred. Key images and miner transactions have their own checks, below. Not re-checked at all: unlock times and the minimum age of spent outputs, the validity of the pricing record a conversion names, the reserve-ratio conditions under which conversions are allowed, and the restriction of transaction types during the audit forks.

Key images. A further program collects the key image of every input on the chain and reports any that occurs twice. A key image is derived from the output that is spent, whatever asset the input claims, so a second spend of one output would show up.

Miner payouts. A third read-only program applies the rule of the node’s validate_miner_transaction to the miner transaction of every block after genesis: its outputs in the base coin must equal the block’s reward minus the reserve share and the yield share, plus the fees paid in the base coin (plus the one-time amount in block 536000), and its outputs in any other asset must equal the fees paid in that asset. The reward of a block is taken from the node’s counter of coins generated and is held against the node’s reward formula without a block-weight penalty, which is the most a block may be given. The program checks amounts per asset; it does not check who receives them (the governance share and its key) or unlock times.

Origin of ring members. For the 49 flagged inputs a fourth program looks up, for every ring member, the transaction that created it, its kind and its public amounts (§5.2, §5.3).

Supply. The emission schedule was recomputed from get_block_reward (cap 2^64 − 1 atomic units, genesis reward 500,000 ZEPH, then (cap − generated) >> 20 per block), and compared with the coins generated per block as the node stored them (§6.3a). The reconciliation of §6.3 uses the database (to block 902083); the one later figure in §6.4 comes from a node’s get_circulating_supply at height 907724 (supply.txt). All 15,040 transactions of blocks 481500 to 535999 were read and their public amount_burnt / amount_minted summed per asset (audit-transactions.tsv, audit-summary.txt).

Limits.

  • Amounts in transactions are hidden. The scan shows that a defect was used and when, never how much.
  • The real spend inside a ring cannot be identified. Where only some ring members have a foreign asset type, neither a spend through defect B nor a harmless decoy is proven (§5.3).
  • A scan can only find forms of abuse it looks for. The chain scan checks the two known defects. The re-validation shows that every transaction passes the checks as they are coded, which a transaction exploiting a defect in the checks themselves, as A and B were, passes as well. Neither is a proof that no third defect existed.
  • Of the project’s posts on X and of its website we read what is listed in §11. Its articles on Medium were not read and are not used.
  • No historical supply table is stored. The reserve and the asset totals at earlier heights were rebuilt from genesis (§6.3a) and agree with every figure they can be compared with.

4. The two defects

4.1 Defect A: range proofs not verified (launch to hard fork 6)

A range proof shows that a hidden amount is not negative. Without it, a transaction can have one output of a huge amount and one of a “negative” amount that cancel in the balance check.

Zephyr’s verification function, as launched (1f3b2f496, rctSigs.cpp L1645-L1652) and unchanged in v2.3.0 for blocks below hard fork 6 (v2.3.0, L1588-L1590 and L1763-L1770):

const bool bulletproof_plus = is_rct_bulletproof_plus(rv.type);
CHECK_AND_ASSERT_MES(bulletproof_plus, false, "Only bulletproofs supported for Stablero");
CHECK_AND_ASSERT_MES(rv.outPk.size() == n_bulletproof_plus_amounts(rv.p.bulletproofs_plus), false, "Mismatched sizes of outPk and bulletproofs_plus");
...
for (size_t i = 0; i < rv.p.bulletproofs.size(); i++)
  proofs.push_back(&rv.p.bulletproofs[i]);

if (!proofs.empty() && !verBulletproof(proofs))

The function requires the Bulletproof+ type, whose proofs are in rv.p.bulletproofs_plus, and then verifies the other vector, rv.p.bulletproofs, which is empty for such a transaction. It was the only semantics check on all three paths (mempool, block, pool re-check).

Origin. Haven’s function of the same name and structure at Zephyr’s fork date (211ddae93, L1667-L1668 and L1983). In Haven’s history the function first accepts Bulletproof+ types, while still verifying only the old vector, in commit 141d4a359ce50001b35df83aa2a26314af4b73cf (2023-04-25, the rebase work onto Monero v0.18); before the rebase (tag v3.0.8) the function required the old Bulletproof type and verified it, which was correct. The omission is therefore present in Haven’s rebase work one month before Zephyr’s first commit contains it. From which block Haven’s own main net ran this function was not established by us.

Fix in Zephyr. Commit 427277f7d (2024-09-29, “Add Zephyr Yield Shares (ZYS) for version 2.0.0 (hardfork v6)”) adds a new function verRctSemanticsZeph, used from hard fork 6, which verifies the right vector (v2.3.0, L1935-L1943). The release notes of v2.0.0 describe the yield feature and do not mention range proofs or a security fix; neither does the announcement of the release on the project’s website (2024-09-30). The project’s own account, given in its Discord on 2025-01-13 (message) stated, Discord:

The 3 transactions from 6 months ago (July 7 2024) stand out because they fail an updated range proofs check relating to bulletproofs/bulletproofs+ that was introduced in Zephyr v2.0.0 as we refactored that section of the code with our Zephyr specific requirements due to ZYS implementation based on Moneros current implementation. [...] The way the check was implemented prior to Zephyr v2.0.0 was incorrect, an oversight on our part. Because these transactions fail the new corrected check it’s implies that an exploit attempt was made.

This agrees with the code on every point we can test: the date, the incorrect check before v2.0.0, and the corrected check in the function added for the yield feature. By this account the correction was a by-product of refactoring, not a fix made in the knowledge of the defect, and the three transactions were noticed later (the first announcement is of 2024-12-29, after Haven’s exploit; §6.1a). Intent cannot be verified from code, but nothing we found contradicts it.

Two further statements on X stated, X. On 2025-01-06 (post): “In our current version, it is no longer possible to submit transactions using the same method that allowed those suspicious transactions to be accepted.” In the post-audit report of 2025-09-06 (article): “we overhauled our codebase and refactored our range-proofs implementation. 5.3 Sixteen transactions with one or two foreign ring members

35 inputs in 16 transactions have a ring of the claimed asset with one (30 inputs) or two (5 inputs) members of another asset: 27 inputs with reserve-coin (ZRS) members in ZEPH rings, 5 with ZSD members, 1 with both, 1 with a yield-coin member, and 1 ZSD input with a ZEPH member. In every case the foreign member is among the four newest of the ring, and it was created either in the first six blocks after a hard fork (89302 to 89306 after fork 3; 360002 to 360005 after fork 6) or 12 to 54 blocks before the spending transaction. One transaction (block 91669, 1975764671c5565220dd8764af9a3ecfab23cacd5f512b349a17bb6c665e30d6, 38 inputs) has such a member in 13 inputs, all created in blocks 89302 to 89305.

Looked at as whole rings (origin.tsv, ring-ages.tsv), these inputs fall into three groups:

  • 29 inputs in 10 transactions, after hard fork 3. The foreign member was created in blocks 89302 to 89306, and no member of any of these rings is newer than block 89321. Yet the transactions are in blocks 89697 to 113049, that is 397 to 23,749 blocks after the fork. The same holds for the other 43 inputs of these ten transactions, which have no foreign member: their rings, too, end by block 89321. A wallet normally takes most decoys from the most recent blocks; here every ring of the transaction was drawn from outputs that end a few blocks after the fork, which is where the first outputs of the new assets are.
  • 3 inputs in 3 transactions, after hard fork 6 (blocks 361396, 361463 and 362266). The foreign member, created in blocks 360002 to 360005, is the newest member of its ring. Two of these transactions have a second input, and its ring is an ordinary recent one (newest members in blocks 361447 and 362250).
  • 3 inputs in 3 transactions (blocks 116489, 119074 and 353852). The ring is an ordinary recent one and the foreign member is its newest, created 54, 25 and 12 blocks earlier: twice a ZSD member in a ZEPH ring, once a ZEPH member in a ZSD ring.

Two readings:

  • Decoys picked across assets, with no gain to anyone. In the first group nothing sets the flagged inputs apart from the other inputs of the same transactions except the foreign member itself: all 72 rings were drawn from the same bounded set of outputs, whose newest end holds the first reserve-coin and stable-coin outputs. We did not establish which software builds such rings. The wallet has selected decoys by asset type since commit 6e98722af (2023-08-11), before hard fork 3, and the mechanism that Monero’s wallet has for bounding decoys at a fork is not active in Zephyr’s source (its fork height is set to 99999999).
  • Real spends through defect B. A ring can be built at will, so this cannot be excluded for any of the sixteen, and it had a motive: in the conversions that created these reserve-coin outputs, right after the fork, one ZEPH bought two reserve coins, so spending such an output as ZEPH would have doubled it. In the second and third group the foreign member is the newest of its ring, the position a real spend often has; that is a heuristic, not proof. The project reports “fewer transactions involving ZRS → ZEPH” stated, Discord, 2025-01-23.

The ring ages show that the ten transactions of the first group share one way of selecting ring members. They do not tell a foreign decoy from a foreign real spend: a list of outputs that was cached at the fork, a transaction prepared then and sent later, a fault in a wallet’s indexing and a deliberately built ring would all look the same. Neither reading is proven for any of the sixteen, and this report does not prefer one. The list is in the evidence files.

5.4 Controls: the whole chain re-checked

The whole database was scanned: blocks 0 to 902083 (the copy ends on 2026-11-07), 1,265,146 transactions, 8,028,931 inputs, 128,462,896 ring members, 1,265,146 range proofs checked, no parse errors. Findings in total: the 3 failing proofs of §5.1 and the 49 inputs of §5.2 and §5.3, nothing else. The last transaction with a foreign ring member is in block 362266; from hard fork 7 (block 433000) on there is none (427,466 transactions from block 435000 on alone), and none of the 604,107 proofs from hard fork 6 on fails. The two rules are enforced by consensus from those forks on, so this part checks the scanner, not the chain.

The re-validation (§2) covered the same 1,265,146 transactions, 33,504 of them conversions (12,428 below hard fork 6). None fails the semantics check, the minted-amount check or the ring-signature check of its height, none passes only under the rules of the previous fork, and none could not be parsed or typed. Every transaction on the chain, the 33 of §5.1 to §5.3 included, therefore passes these checks as the released code applies them at its height: those 33 were accepted because of what the checks did not cover, not because a check was bypassed. For these checks it also follows that no earlier version of the software accepted a transaction that v2.3.0 would refuse.

The key images of all 8,028,931 inputs are distinct (keyimages.cpp, §2): no output was spent twice, under one asset name or under two.

The miner transactions were checked separately (coinbase.cpp, §2). In each of the 902,083 blocks after genesis the outputs equal, to the atomic unit and per asset, the block’s reward minus the reserve and yield shares plus the fees; block 536000 carries the one-time 1,921,650 ZEPH on top. No block’s reward is above the emission formula; 5,412 blocks have a lower one, which is what the block-weight penalty does. So no block paid out more than reward and fees allow.

What these controls leave out is listed in §2. They show that the chain follows the rules as they are coded for the things that decide how many coins exist; they are not a replay of every consensus rule.

6.2 How the audit worked code, chain

Amounts cannot be audited from outside. The audit forks therefore accepted only “audit transactions”: a holder spends old-type outputs and receives new-type outputs, and the transaction states the amount in the open (amount_burnt of the old type equals amount_minted of the new type; this held for all 15,040). The supply tables were rebuilt from these statements. From hard fork 11 the old asset types are rejected.

This establishes how many coins were presented. It does not establish which of them had been emitted: a coin that was never emitted and is moved through an audit transaction is an audited coin. With hidden amounts and histories no audit of this kind can do more. What the excess means. The accounting result needs no assumption: at block 536000 the supply table holds 2,067,576 ZEPH more than ordinary emission to that block, 1,921,650 of it issued by the one-time mint. What it says about earlier coins rests on the project’s statement that the mint replaced an existing balance that could not be audited (§6.4). On that statement the figure is a net one: coins honoured that were never emitted, minus emitted coins that were never audited (lost keys, holders who missed both windows). The first quantity is then at least 2,067,576 and higher by the unknown second. Both parts rest on public data: the 1,921,650 of the one-time mint is a constant in the code and an output on the chain, and the 145,926 follows from the audited amounts, the coins generated and the rebuilt reserve.

Where the non-emitted coins were cannot be decided from the chain. The two limiting cases, both on the project’s statement about that balance:

  • the unaudited exchange balance consisted almost entirely of them, and what the other holders audited was almost entirely emitted coins (their excess is only 145,926);
  • the exchange balance consisted of emitted coins, in which case it belongs inside the 5,019,225, and about two million non-emitted ZEPH were among the coins other holders audited.

Anything between is possible, and this report does not choose. An exchange holds what its customers deposited; nothing in this evidence establishes anything about the exchange’s conduct.

  • Accounting: the amount is added to the block’s base reward and so to the counter of generated coins. The base reward, by the emission formula on that counter, fell from 10.27 ZEPH in block 536000 to 8.43 ZEPH in block 536001, about 18 % less. The lead over the original schedule was 2,067,576 ZEPH at block 536000 and about 1,494,000 at height 907724 (2026-11-14, supply.txt). It keeps shrinking only while rewards follow the emission curve. The reward has a floor of 0.6 ZEPH per block, the tail reward; the mint brings the moment that floor is reached forward, and once both schedules pay the floor the difference no longer changes. A projection without block-weight penalties puts that at block 3,307,370 with the mint and 3,513,570 without it, about ten years after the audit, with 123,720 ZEPH of the mint still ahead of the original schedule. So lower rewards offset about 1.8 million of the mint, not all of it, and the 145,926 are not offset at all. Lower rewards mean less for miners, for the reserve share and for the yield share; the minted coins themselves stay in the supply.

6.5 The other assets

ZSD, ZRS and ZYS have no emission schedule; they arise from conversions, whose amounts are public. Rebuilt from those (§6.3a), 262,688 units are missing after the audit. The project’s estimate of 2025-06-11 against its reported supply stated, Discord: ZSD −106,320.68, ZRS −146,210.32, ZYS −4,103.28, together 256,634 units fewer than reported. It adds (2025-05-07): “The other assets look like they will come in under expected, and we know that some of those assets were likely morphed into ZEPH.”

What follows for the two defects (inference). Under defect B one unit of another asset becomes one ZEPH and the old count of that asset keeps it. A unit that was morphed into ZEPH and stayed there therefore shows up as a unit missing at the audit, next to units that were simply not audited: units missing = net morphing into ZEPH + units not audited. (Morphing the other way would offset it; one of the sixteen partial rings runs that way, §5.3.) On this accounting, and with the totals of the other assets being what the public conversions add up to, defect B can explain at most about 262,688 ZEPH of the excess (the units missing by our rebuild, §6.3a; the project’s estimate of June was 256,634); the project’s own best guess for morphing is about 200,000. Of the excess of 2,067,576 at least about 1.8 million is therefore not explained by defect B. The only other known defect is A, and three transactions with invalid range proofs exist; but the chain does not show what they created, so we do not attribute the remainder to them. It is unexplained by B, and A could explain it.

The project’s post-audit report leaves the same question open stated, X, 2025-09-06. Of the range-proof transactions: “We were not able to determine if or how much net chain-wide inflation originated from this vector, but the possibility that this contributed could also not be ruled out.” Of the outcome: the other assets “fell below pre‑audit expectations; consistent with ZSD/ZRS → ZEPH morphing”. The report gives the deltas (ZEPH +2,056,803; ZSD −106,320.68; ZRS −146,210.32; ZYS −4,103.28) and does not say what accounts for the part of the ZEPH figure that morphing cannot.

8. How the incident was handled

Observations, not a verdict. This report does not judge who is to blame; the weighing is left to the reader.

What the project did.

  • It made public announcements at each of these steps: the three transactions (2024-12-29), the deposit stop (2025-01-06), defect B with its own count and estimate two days after the hotfix, the expected inflation before the audit closed (2025-05-07), and the mint with its amount and the change to emission (2025-06-11).
  • It published its own post-audit report (2025-08-27 on its website, 2025-09-06 on X), with the figures and with sections on what went well and what did not, and it names the origin of both defects (§3).
  • Both defects are closed. The chain kept producing blocks throughout, while ordinary transfers and conversions were off for 54,500 blocks (§6.1). Defect A was closed by the v2.0.0 release, by the project’s account before it knew of the defect (§4.1); defect B by a hotfix in the month in which, by the project’s account, it was reported (“Jan 2025”) stated, X, 2025-07-28.
  • The audit produced, for the first time, supply figures that anyone can verify from the chain.
  • The emission parameter was not raised: the one-time mint was booked as emission and is, for the larger part, offset by lower rewards in the years after (§6.4).
  • The project wanted the exchange balance audited on chain and called minting it “a last resort” (§6.4).
  • The accounting can be reproduced from public source code and a public chain, which is what made this review possible. The attribution of the mint and the check of the balance are the project’s statements.

What stays open.

  • Defect A was live for sixteen and a half months and defect B for almost sixteen. On 2025-01-06 the project wrote that its work “includes a comprehensive review of our entire codebase, alongside the already planned audit” stated, X; we found no account of the outcome of such a review. A search of the project’s Discord server and of its X account on 2026-11-14 returned none, and none of the 34 announcements from that date to the end of 2025 reports on one (§11). Asked on 2025-08-25 whether there would be a third-party review of the code, it answered: “None are planned, but they aren’t ruled out!”, and said it had placed its trust in the audits of Haven’s code (§3) stated, X.
  • In July 2026 the project announced “We have selected Sherlock for the external audit” stated, Discord, 2026-07-14. That audit concerns the project’s EVM bridge. The scope the project published names “off-chain bridge repository, API, watchers and relayers” and “Solidity smart contracts” stated, Discord, community-funding, 2026-07-28; the node’s consensus code is not in it. The project had said so beforehand. Asked whether the audit would also look at the base chain, an account with the team role “Marketing Lead” answered: “Scoped to the EVM bridge only: the smart contracts plus the off-chain bridge API/watchers. Not the L1.”, and added: “That’s not to say we’ll never look at the L1... as the protocol scales, a dedicated L1-focused review is a reasonable step” stated, Discord, general, 2026-07-08. The first round of the bridge audit was reported complete on 2026-08-10 stated, Discord. We found no third-party review of Zephyr’s node code: not in the project’s statements, and not in its public repositories of the node and the wallets, which hold no audit report and no reference to one (checked 2026-11-14).
  • The correction of defect A in v2.0.0 was not announced as a security fix at the time; by the project’s account it was not known to be one. The project disclosed the substance afterwards: on 2024-12-29 as a possibility, on 2025-01-13 in full (“incorrect, an oversight on our part”), and on 2025-01-23 as distinct from asset morphing. The fullest of these statements is in the general chat, not in an announcement or the release notes.
  • The project’s estimate for defect B is about 200,000 ZEPH; the excess is about 2.07 million. The project’s post-audit report says it could not determine how much came from the range-proof transactions, so what accounts for the difference is open by its own account as well (§6.5).
  • The audit could not remove coins that had not been emitted and were presented; it turned them into audited coins. That is a limit of the method, even if every holder takes part (§6.2).
  • Holders who did not move their coins in the two windows lost them. Their number is unknown.
  • The one-time mint is 1,921,650 ZEPH, a quarter of everything emitted by then, created by a constant in the source and paid to a project-designated address. The amount was fixed in the release before the project checked the balance from the exchange’s wallet seed; the check, the identity of the wallet and the payout rest on the project’s word and cannot be verified on chain. Its cost falls on miners and on the reserve and yield shares of later blocks.
  • We found no independent post-mortem; the public account we could read consists of the project’s posts, its own post-audit report and the release notes.

9. State of the code on 2026-11-14 code

  • Both defects are closed in v2.3.0, the current release. Blocks below hard fork 6 are, necessarily, still validated with the old function; that affects history only.
  • The public repository’s only branch ends at the v2.3.0 commit of 2025-06-10; the release was published the next day. There is no later commit and no later release (checked 2026-11-14).
