## 3. Protocol History and Evolution_
🛡️ 3.1. The Battle-Tested Narrative (2023-2024 Drawdown) 
In late 2023 and early 2024, Zephyr experienced a classic crypto boom-and-bust cycle where its market valuation outpaced its adoption, leading to a market correction and a subsequent price drawdown of over 95% for the base asset, $ZEPH.
Crucially, during this period of extreme stress, the $ZSD stablecoin maintained its peg to the US dollar without issue. This event served as an "inadvertent" protocol stress test and a powerful, real-world validation of the Djed model's resilience. While other protocols have collapsed under similar pressure, Zephyr's hard-coded collateralization rules worked as designed, automatically halting $ZSD minting and preventing a death spiral.

———
🛡️ 3.2. The Supply Integrity Audit (Late 2024 - Mid-2025)
This period was a defining test of the protocol's security and the team's commitment to transparency.
Initial Discovery (Dec 2024)
3 suspicious transactions that resembled an exploit found on the Haven protocol were found, raising concerns about potential unauthorized minting.
Second Exploit Identified (Jan 2025)
An anonymous developer discovered and reported a separate bug that allowed for "asset morphing"—where one asset (like $ZSD) could be spent as another ($ZEPH) with modified wallet code. This exploit was confirmed to have caused inflation of the $ZEPH supply. The bug was immediately patched via a hotfix hardfork (v2.0.2).
The On-Chain Audit (Mar - Jun 2025)
To determine the precise impact of these exploits and restore confidence, the team initiated a mandatory, full-scale on-chain audit. All users and exchanges were required to move their funds through a special transaction type to have them recognized on the new, post-audit chain. The process was delayed by the need to grant an extension to the exchange MEXC, which had the largest user wallet and had issues completing the process on time.
Audit Conclusion and Resolution (Jun 2025)
The audit concluded with Hardfork v11 (v2.3.0). It confirmed a final circulating supply of ~$9.7M $ZEPH, an inflation of ~2.39M $ZEPH compared to the expected emission schedule. The supplies of $ZSD and $ZRS were correspondingly lower, confirming a portion had been morphed into $ZEPH. To counteract this, the team adjusted the emission curve forward, effectively placing the protocol at the end of its third year of emissions to ensure the 18.4M max supply cap remains intact. The saga ended with the protocol having a verifiably accurate supply and being described as supremely battle-tested; ready for an effective relaunch.

TITLE: Zephyr's Chain Reconciliation: Post-Audit Report

## Summary
Between late 2024 and mid‑2025, Zephyr undertook an unprecedented chain‑wide audit in response to concerns raised after exploits uncovered on other Monero-based forks and our own subsequent investigations. Two distinct issues drove our actions:
- Cross‑asset ring member bug ("morphing" bug): Prior to v2.0.2 (HF7), consensus did not strictly enforce that ring members match the spending output’s asset_type. With modified wallet code, this allowed some transactions to spend inputs of one asset as if they were another (e.g., ZSD or ZRS spent as ZEPH). Thirty transactions were identified with this characteristic, and analysis suggested a material—but bounded—amount of ZSD (and some ZRS) was morphed 1:1 into ZEPH.
- Range‑proof anomalies: Three historical transactions failed under updated range‑proof checks. While we did not seem to experience the effects of a catastrophic chain‑wide inflation, these transactions prevented us from asserting perfect finality over historical supply without a chain‑level reconciliation.
## Response:
- We executed an emergency hotfix fork (v2.0.2 / HF7 at block 433,000, Jan 2025) enforcing asset‑type matching in rings, closing the morphing vector going forward.
- We designed and executed an on‑chain Audit hardfork process (v2.1.0 / HF8 at block 481,500, Mar 31, 2025) that temporarily disabled normal transfers and enabled special audit transactions per asset (ZEPH, ZSD, ZRS, ZYS). Coins not audited would be invalidated in a subsequent fork.
- Due to partner operational constraints, we extended the audit window (v2.2.0 / HF10 at block 511,000, May 9, 2025).
- We completed the migration, restored normality, and moved supply accounting to the audited asset types (v2.3.0 / HF11 at block 536,000, June 11, 2025). Because one major exchange (MEXC) could not audit its entire custodial balance in time, HF11 included a one‑time mint of 1,921,650 ZEPH to reconcile their unauditable balance.
## Outcome:
- Final reported ZEPH supply increased versus the pre‑audit trajectory (roughly aligning with an effective shift to end‑of‑Year‑3 emission), while ZSD, ZRS, and ZYS supplies fell below pre‑audit expectations; consistent with ZSD/ZRS → ZEPH morphing.
- Old (unaudited) asset types are now invalid; normal functionality is restored.
- We retained DJED reserve integrity, exchange order book sanity, and ecosystem continuity.
Bottom line: The audit was disruptive, but necessary. It closed a consensus hole, reconciled supply with evidence‑backed on‑chain attestations, and reset Zephyr on a robust, auditable footing.
# Incident #1: Cross‑Asset Ring Member Bug ("Morphing")
## What Happened
Prior to HF7, ring signatures did not enforce that all ring members shared the same asset_type as the spent output. With custom wallet code, inputs from ZSD/ZRS could be presented in a ring for a ZEPH spend, enabling 1:1 morphing into ZEPH at validation time.
## Scope & Data Points
- 30 transactions exhibited cross‑asset ring members beginning soon after v1.0.0 (Sep 2023, when multi‑asset support launched).
- Pattern analysis inferred ~95k ZSD morphed in 10 of those transactions and an upper bound in the low‑hundreds‑of‑thousands of ZSD/ZRS across the remainder.
- Most morphing appeared to be ZSD → ZEPH, rational given pricing differentials.
## User‑Visible Effects
No immediate market chaos manifested. DJED reserve metrics, exchange order books, and balances remained within expected ranges, implying the morphing volume, while material, was absorbable by the market and/or dispersed over time.
## Fix
HF7 (v2.0.2) enforced strict asset‑type matching for ring members at consensus, preventing further morphing attempts.
# Incident #2: Range‑Proof Anomalies (3 Tx)
## What Happened
Three historical transactions failed under updated range-proof verification. During the Zephyr Protocol v2.0.0 development cycle, which enabled the ZSD Yield Mechanism and introduced ZYS (Zephyr Yield Share), we overhauled our codebase and refactored our range-proofs implementation. When these new stricter checks were applied to our historical chain during the audit investigation, the three pre-existing transactions did not validate under the new rules.
## Risk & Context
We were not able to determine if or how much net chain-wide inflation originated from this vector, but the possibility that this contributed could also not be ruled out. In addition, their existence prevented a mathematically clean historical reconciliation leaving doubt and uncertainty.
## Status
This is distinct from the cross-asset ring-member “morphing” bug fixed at HF7 (v2.0.2). The audit forks (HF8/HF10/HF11) resolved historical ambiguity by migrating supply to audited asset types and invalidating unaudited coins.
# Why a Chain Audit?
A Chain audit was the only practical way to produce an explicit, on‑chain snapshot of legitimate supply and ownership.
# Audit Steps:
1. Freeze normal activity and enable deterministic audit transactions per asset.
2. Require every holder (including reserves and exchanges) to re‑attest balances on‑chain.
3. Invalidate all unaudited coins.
4. Migrate accounting to new, audited asset types with determined supply figures
# Timeline
- Dec 29, 2024: Public disclosure of investigations; exchanges asked to disable deposits while we prepared the audit plan.
- Jan 21, 2025: HF7 (v2.0.2) announced.
- Jan 23, 2025: Post‑fork details: bug fixed; 30 cross‑asset ring transactions identified; preliminary morphing estimates shared; continued audit work.
- Feb 19, 2025: Audit HF announced for Mar 31.
- Mar 31, 2025: HF8 (v2.1.0) activated at block 481,500; normal transfers paused; audit tx types enabled.
- Apr 1–May 7, 2025: Audit window opened; CLI + GUI/Web tooling rolled out; periodic progress reports shared.
- May 9, 2025: HF10 (v2.2.0) audit extension at block 511,000 to accommodate a major exchange’s operational backlog.
- May 12, 2025: Extension live; final push to completion.
- Jun 11, 2025: HF11 (v2.3.0) activated at block 536,000; normal network restored; one‑time 1,921,650 ZEPH mint for MEXC unauditable balance; supply accounting switched to audited assets.
- Jun 16, 2025: HF11 success update; explorers & wallets catching up; on‑chain supply figures published.
# Impact Assessment
## Economic
- ZEPH supply: Final audited ZEPH exceeded the pre‑audit emission trajectory. We adjusted the emission schedule such that the network is effectively at the end of Year 3 for ZEPH emission while preserving the 18.4M max supply (pre‑tail).
- ZSD/ZRS/ZYS: Supplies came in below pre‑audit expectations, consistent with some ZSD/ZRS having been morphed into ZEPH historically.
- MEXC reconciliation: A one‑time mint (1,921,650 ZEPH) was included in HF11 to reconcile their custodial balance that could not be fully audited within the window.

## Operational
- Exchanges were temporarily offline for deposits/withdrawals; partners coordinated audit transactions at scale.
- Two additional forks (HF10/HF11) were required to accommodate operational realities and finalize the migration.
# Security & Correctness
- The morphing vector is permanently closed.
- Historical ambiguity from the 3 range‑proof‑anomalous transactions is removed by migration to audited asset types and invalidation of unaudited coins.
# What Went Well
- Rapid remediation: HF7 closed the morphing vector quickly after discovery.
- Transparent comms: Frequent public updates; clear instructions for nodes, pools, exchanges, and users (CLI + GUI/Web); Updates on audit progress and audited supply.
- Deterministic audit design: Using our native-asset conversion framework to create audit transaction types enabled a clean, on-chain attestation process.
- DJED resilience: Throughout the audit and beyond, DJED reserve integrity was maintained.
# What Didn’t Go Well
- Complexity & delay: The audit took longer than initial hopes due to scope expansion (morphing bug) and partner throughput.
- UX friction: Large wallets (especially with many small inputs) hit splitting constraints; we had to iterate the tooling and provide workarounds.
- Partner dependency: A single large custodian’s backlog required an extension and a bespoke reconciliation (mint), delaying completion and complicating optics.

Some valid points raised; it's worth taking the time to address each of them.

Regarding the suggestion that past issues weren't clearly addressed: We disclosed the morphing bug publicly in Dec 2024, posted updates throughout every stage of the 6-month remediation, and published a full postmortem documenting what went well and what didn't.

Full Audit Postmortem:

https://zephyrprotocol.com/blog/2025-08-27-Zephyr-Audit-Postmortem…

The technical details:
Two distinct issues: cross-asset ring member bug ("morphing") where ZSD/ZRS could be spent as ZEPH — 30 transactions identified, ~95k ZSD morphed in 10 of those.
And 3 historical transactions that failed under updated range-proof checks, where potential inflation couldn't be confirmed or ruled out.

The response:
4 hardforks over 6 months. Emergency fix closing the vector (Jan 2025). Full chain pause with audit transactions for every asset (Mar 2025). Extension for exchange backlogs (May 2025). Final migration restoring normal operations (Jun 2025). Every holder and exchange re-attested balances. Unaudited coins were permanently invalidated.

Post-audit supply deltas vs. pre-audit reported:
ZEPH: +2,056,803 (includes one-time 1,921,650 mint for MEXC's unauditable custodial balance) 
ZSD: −106,320 
ZRS: −146,210 
ZYS: −4,103

Zephyr is a Monero-based privacy chain — individual balances and transaction amounts are not publicly visible. That's by design. Supply reconciliation was achieved through the audit process itself, where every holder attested balances via special audit transactions. The methodology and final figures are documented in the postmortem linked above.

Was the process as smooth as we had hoped?
No. It was disruptive, took longer than planned, large wallets hit UX friction, and one exchange couldn't complete in time. The postmortem documents all of this. There has never been an effort to deny past challenges.
---

The 95% drop and if the system was 'truly stressed':
This misunderstands how Djed works. 
The peg mechanism is not liquidity-dependent. It's not an AMM pool that can be drained. It's not a redemption queue that breaks under volume. 

The reserve ratio is checked mathematically before every single mint and redeem operation. When it hits a threshold, the protocol halts that operation. It doesn't matter if there are 50 users or 50,000 — the circuit breaker triggers at the same ratio either way. 

More liquidity doesn't make the breakers work better. They work identically at any scale. What changes with scale is the experience of hitting a breaker — more users means more people temporarily unable to redeem. 
That's a real tradeoff, but the peg doesn't break, because the protocol will not execute a transaction that would push reserves below safety thresholds. 

The math was in the Djed whitepaper. The 95% crash proved it in production. 
We can only point to things that actually happened — and what happened is the peg held under the most severe conditions ZEPH has faced. 
Dismissing that because the ecosystem was smaller is asking for a stress test while rejecting the results of one.
---

On "safety is not just math”:
Agreed. However, Zephyr's design eliminates several of the usual trust assumptions. There is no admin key, no multisig, no governance committee, no freeze function. Any protocol change requires a hardfork with miner consensus. This is architectural, not a policy choice.

The protocol also has a built-in defense against price manipulation through dual pricing. Every block carries both a spot price and a moving average. 
When you mint ZSD or ZRS, you pay the higher of the two. When you redeem, you receive the lower. 

This means pumping ZEPH on a thin order book to mint cheap ZSD doesn't work — you'd be minting at the inflated price, not profiting from it. 
The same logic prevents dump-and-redeem attacks in reverse.
---

To be transparent about what isn't decentralized yet: 
The V1 oracle that provides these price feeds is centralized — 3 oracle nodes operated by the team, with signed pricing records embedded in every block. It works, it's battle-tested, and the dual spot/MA mechanism prevents gaming. Granted, it's a centralized component and we don't pretend otherwise.

A fully decentralized Oracle V2 is on the roadmap: 
https://x.com/zephyr_org/status/1964638320987660597… 
It becomes critical as the protocol scales, and the team has been upfront that it's a priority for long-term credibility, not an afterthought.
---

Liquidity/Market size:
You’re right. Currently Zephyr sits at ~$5M market cap, with ~$400K ZSD circ. 
This is a currently small ecosystem and we don't claim otherwise. 
Growth is the challenge; peg mechanics are not. Those are separate problems and we treat them as such.
